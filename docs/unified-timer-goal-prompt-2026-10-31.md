## Product contract

- A timer contains one or more ordered steps. A single step is directly editable; multiple steps use compact rows. Each step has a duration, name, ending cue, alarm behavior and optional sub-bells. Repetition applies to the entire ordered round. Do not introduce nested step repetition.
- Reuse actual Chandas components, themes, icons, duration inputs, sound controls, schedule editors and accessibility behavior. Replace the experimental entry with a coherent normal application flow once integration is verified. Retire duplicated prototype scaffolding when it is no longer needed.
- Essential settings stay in the main editing sheet. Optional capabilities are discoverable through a horizontally scrolling, labelled action row. Enabling an option reveals its current value and editing controls in the main sheet. Enabled options remain visible after selecting other actions, switching configurations and reopening. Do not replace this row with More or a wrapping grid.
- Sheets support dragging between partial and expanded heights and dismissing, with explicit expand/collapse/close equivalents. Keep scrolling, dragging, keyboard entry and dismissal reliable. Keep the configuration name and save controls readily accessible. Avoid simultaneous duplicate editing modals and unintended actions when closing a sheet. Preserve reduced motion, e-ink, safe areas and accessible targets.
- The configuration name opens an expandable picker of compact configuration cards. Support Save (update this configuration), Save as, rename, duplicate, delete with confirmation, import/export and the existing running-selector visibility preference. Each configuration has a saved snapshot and one durable draft. Dirty switching offers Save & switch, Keep draft & switch, and Discard & switch; cancelling preserves the current selection. Restore kept drafts, including unnamed drafts, after switching and restart. A library-management action must not silently discard another configuration's edits.
- Preserve the existing running watchface, layout and immediate controls. Adapt its data, step/sub-bell display and secondary editing surfaces where integration requires it, without a separate visual redesign. Maintain pause/resume, previous/next, restart, snap, alarm once/locked and step alarms, mute by iteration or duration, Focus, sound levels, hiding/revealing controls and floating-timer handoff.

## Preserve the full timer behavior

Inventory every existing control and behavior before replacing it, and maintain a coverage ledger. Preserve exact-second durations; enabled/disabled sub-bell layers and tracks; cadence and selected occurrences; randomized gaps; independent after-start and before-end cues; colors; display visibility independent of audibility; one or multiple sounds with ordered/random playback; shared imported sounds; per-cue volume and haptic overrides; master sound/vibration settings; alarms and their sound, volume and duration; continuous, round-count and elapsed-duration bounds; terminal gong replacement; whole-round clock alignment; Active/Quiet windows; Focus; call mute; notifications/live countdown; floating display preferences; themes and precision preferences. Preserve relevant limits, unavailable-sound fallbacks, import warnings and permission recovery flows.

Sub-bells must run correctly inside every sequence step, not merely appear in the editor. Update the TypeScript domain/timeline, Kotlin scheduler, persisted native state, bridge capabilities and validation together. Derive step-relative cue times from the authoritative session. Ensure deterministic random schedules agree across runtimes and survive pause, restore and process recreation. Preserve collision and terminal-boundary rules, quiet gates, alarm dismissal and silent step skipping. Keep display countdowns and overlay cue responses consistent with hidden-bell behavior.

Migrate existing Cycle/Pattern and Sequence configurations, presets, settings and sessions safely to versioned unified records. A Cycle becomes one step with its existing bells; a Sequence retains its step order, durations, cues and settings. Keep useful legacy records recoverable, make migration idempotent, and handle interrupted writes and unsupported native versions explicitly. Do not wipe real application data or treat experimental prototype records as production truth. Saved configuration drafts and temporary live-session state must remain distinct; editing a draft must not silently replace a running timer.
